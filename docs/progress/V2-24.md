# V2-24 — Enhanced style: AMD-055 v1.4 (local-camera floor, AO ceiling)

Lot V2-24 (R3, clean side). AMD-055 goes to **v1.4** and closes both tickets of lot V2-23:

- **Q-096 → answered.** The provisional choice is right for the ordinary camera (`+Y`, close-up R
  unchanged), but the camera's vertical is wrong in local-camera mode too: the "Punch Out!!"
  profile of 3dSen 2.0 tilts its local camera by **−60°**, where
  `dot((0, 0, −1), q · (0, 1, 0))` = 0.866 < 0.9, and the reference still reflects the mat.
  **In local-camera mode the vertical is the image-plane normal facing the camera**, `(0, 0, −1)`
  in the scene frame before curvature, whatever the tilt; the extent is measured in the `xy`
  plane.
- **Q-097 → answered.** Hypothesis 1 confirmed: the reference is **not** under the
  `0.1 × ao.strength` ceiling. The ceiling becomes `O ≤ min(0.95 ; 0.3 × ao.strength)` (0.9 by
  default) and the contact invariant of close-up A becomes « strictly below 1 and ≥ **0.10** », to
  one sRGB level. Recalibrate both modes on the Q-095 reference figures (area **and** mean
  darkening), the band staying soft — continuous fall-off, but deep at contact.

## Notes

### Measurement method (V2-23's, unchanged)

Captures downscaled to 960 × 540 (box filter). Two figures per capture:

- **zone** = share of pixels whose sum over RGB of |effect − base| exceeds 12;
- **mean darkening** = mean over the whole 960 × 540 image and the three channels of
  (base − effect), in 0-255 levels.

Scenes: Punch-Out!! (`Mike Tyson's Punch-O [Japan][USA][!!].nes`, profile "Punch Out!!",
`--run 1501` with the lot's start presses) and Super Mario Bros. (`[Europe][!]`, `--run 419`),
both `--size 2560x1440 --style 4`. The comparison tool lives in the session scratchpad.

**What the ratio columns say, and it is the whole of Q-097's answer in one line.** Both modes read
a darkest 1 % of **0.69** and a darkest 5 % of **0.70** — the signature of a population *clipped*
against the old ceiling `0.1 × ao.strength` = 0.3, not of a distribution. The reference, at the
same settings, reads 0.13 / 0.42 (Depth) and 0.16 / 0.35 (MSVO): it goes **five times deeper at
the contact** than the old ceiling even permits. Raising the ceiling to
`min(0.95 ; 0.3 × ao.strength)` = 0.9 is therefore not a free parameter to be spent on more area —
it has to be spent on **depth**, and the gain of each mode has to come *down* by roughly the same
factor so that the area does not run away with it. The two figures to land are
`under 0.70` ≈ 11.8 % (Depth) / 19.8 % (MSVO) and `darkest 1 %` ≈ 0.13 / 0.16.

Framing-adjusted targets (×1.23 on area, and on the whole-image mean, which scales with it):
Depth **28.7 % / 6.4**, MSVO **39.6 % / 8.7**.

### Step 2 — Q-096 applied: the floor vertical no longer depends on the camera

```
up = Camera.LocalCamera ? (0, 0, −1) : (0, 1, 0)
```

One line of `SceneRenderer.FloorVertical`, and `CameraView` drops out of its signature
altogether — which is the substance of the answer: *neither* mode reads the camera's attitude.
`FloorPlaneAxes` needed no change: seeded from the scene frame's `Z`, a vertical of `+Y` gives
back exactly `(X, Z)` — the `xz` of the amendment's wording — and a vertical of `(0, 0, −1)`
falls through to the `X` seed and gives `(−Y, X)`, i.e. the **`xy` plane** v1.4 names for the
local camera. The generalisation stays strict at both ends.

| ring mat, local camera, rx = **−60°** | (0, 0, −1) | (0, 0, −1) | **1.000** | yes | yes (reference) |

### Step 3 — the ceiling, and what it did to the gain

`SceneState.OcclusionCeiling` becomes `min(0.95, 0.3 × AoStrength)`: 0.3 / 0.6 / 0.9 / 0.95 /
0.95, 0.9 at the default. Nothing else in either estimator had to change — the ceiling is the
apply pass's single uniform in both modes.

#### The gain goes back to where V2-22 put it, and that is the finding of the step

Raising the ceiling alone (share kept at V2-23's 0.06) gives Punch-Out!! **41.3 % / 16.00**
against a framing-adjusted target of 28.7 % / 6.4, with the darkest 5 % of the frame at **0.11**
against the reference's 0.42: a wall-to-wall saturated plateau, which is precisely the
« bande à fond plat et à bord franc » Q-097's own first section says a soft band does not do. So
the ceiling had to be spent on depth and the gain brought back down. Sweep, Punch-Out!! then SMB
1-1, at `ao.*` default:

| 0.06 (V2-23) | 41.28 % / 16.000 | 0.10 / 0.11 | 33.1 % | 9.95 % / 2.547 |
| 0.09 | 40.04 % / 15.280 | 0.10 / 0.12 | 32.5 % | 9.08 % / 2.401 |
| 0.12 | 38.76 % / 13.750 | 0.10 / 0.14 | 31.2 % | 8.42 % / 2.196 |
| 0.18 | 37.15 % / 11.427 | 0.10 / 0.20 | 28.1 % | 7.44 % / 1.897 |
| 0.25 | 35.31 % / 9.122 | 0.12 / 0.29 | 23.7 % | 6.64 % / 1.626 |

**0.35 is exactly the constant lot V2-22 derived** — the blocked share measured at a right-angle
corner, so that an ordinary deep corner is what reaches the ceiling. Two tickets of compensation
took it to 0.06 and this lot brings it back unchanged, which is the cleanest possible evidence
that v1.4's ceiling is the right reading: the whole of Q-095's « environ trois fois trop faible »
was the old ceiling of 0.3 clipping a population the reference takes down to 0.13, and V2-23's
six-fold gain was fighting that clip rather than calibrating the estimator. The law, the kernel
and the radius were right all along.

At 0.35 the mean lands within 6 % of the framing-adjusted target and the darkest 5 % of the frame
on the reference's 0.42 exactly. What is left over is shape, not magnitude: our darkest 1 % is
0.20 against 0.13 and our under-0.70 share 18.2 % against 11.8 %, i.e. our band is a little
broader and a little less peaked than the reference's. `zaxRiseRamp` was swept as the candidate
lever for that (0.25 → 0.50 at three shares) on the reasoning that a wider ramp damps the ring
mat's shallow staircase relief while leaving true corners at full weight; at equal mean it moves
the darkest-1 %/5 % ratio from 0.48 to 0.45 — nothing. **V2-23's 0.25 therefore stands
unchanged**, and the residual shape difference is left as it is rather than tuned for.

SMB 1-1 overshoots its reference figures by about 3× in both. That is the frame mismatch V2-23
already recorded — the answer's own measurement of the same commit was 2.4× below this harness's
figure for the same scene — not a property of the calibration.

#### Q-098: v1.4 updated one invariant of §2.4 and not its neighbour

Applying the ceiling made the **third** tiret of §2.4 fail in both modes. It still reads
« `ao.strength` = 5 : le rapport est partout ≥ 0,50 ; `ao.strength` = 1 : partout ≥ 0,90 » —
which is `1 − 0.1 × ao.strength` to the digit, the ceiling of **v1.3**. The **second** tiret, the
one v1.4 did move, now brings the contact of close-up A to 0.10 at the default strength; under
the proportionality of *Force* that same contact reads 0.70 at strength 1, under the 0.90 the
third tiret demands, and 0.05 at strength 5, under its 0.50. The two tirets exclude each other.

A second, smaller consequence: the new ceiling is **constant** between strengths 4 and 5 (0.95
both), so under the « plafond comme échelle » reading that Q-095's answer confirmed those two
settings produce identical captures and an **equal** contact — where the same tiret asks for
« monotone décroissant ».

Ticket **Q-098** records both. Provisional choice: the third tiret follows the ceiling it
restates, `1 − min(0.95 ; 0.3 × ao.strength)` to one sRGB level; and « monotone décroissant » is
read at large, the word « strictement » being absent, as the fourth tiret of the same list says
of `ao.thickness` that the ratio « ne **croît** pas ». The strict decrease is still asserted over
strengths 1 to 4, where the ceiling does rise, so the criterion loses no discriminating power.
Encoded as a named `Ceiling(strength)` helper used by both modes' encodings.

accessibility, unscaled — already measures 6.38 on this frame, where V2-23 had to divide by 0.12

clipping it.

against 23.3 %, a factor 1.38), and here the two modes are level in area at every gain — 32.20 %
against 32.86 %, a factor 0.98. That is a property of the port's spatial footprint, not of the
gain: no value in the sweep moves the area ratio past 1.04.

#### A conformance defect the ceiling exposed: the Low quality had two pyramid levels

`REQ_SCN_083_the_three_qualities_agree_in_msvo` failed on the new calibration, with a spread of
**0.328** at the contact of close-up A against a window of 0.10. The three qualities measured
0.6151 / 0.3067 / 0.2871: **Low was half as dark**, not noisier. (The Depth mode, for comparison,
measured 0.425 / 0.441 / 0.401 — a spread of 0.04.)

The cause is `LevelsByQuality = [2, 3, 3]`. The coarsest level of a multi-scale estimate is what
carries its wide half, and a contact against an 8-unit cube at `r` = 20 is mostly wide; dropping
that level took half of Low's darkening. Under the ceiling of v1.3 the same gap was a sixth of an
sRGB level and no lot had seen it. REQ-SCN-083 wants of the three qualities « le même aspect
général, Low pouvant être plus bruité » — noisier, not lighter — and declares the number of
scales and samples **unobservable**, so this is a defect in its own right, exposed by the
calibration rather than caused by it (as V2-23's scope breach was).

Fixed as `[3, 3, 3]`: the three now agree to **0.061** (0.3484 / 0.3067 / 0.2871), and Low stays
the cheapest quality by its sample pattern — four entries of the checkerboard against seven, at
half resolution. The default quality is Medium, which already had three levels, so the
calibration above is unaffected.

### Left open

- **Q-098** — v1.4 moved the second invariant of §2.4 and left the third quoting the ceiling of
  v1.3; and the new ceiling, being constant between strengths 4 and 5, makes strict monotony at
  the contact arithmetically impossible. Both read provisionally as described in step 3.
- The reference's band stays **more peaked** than this one: darkest 1 % 0.13 against 0.20, share
  under 0.70 11.8 % against 18.2 %. `zaxRiseRamp` was swept as the shape lever and moves the
  peakedness by 0.03 at equal mean, so nothing was tuned for it.
- The reference has MSVO **wider** than Depth on the ring by a factor 1.38; the port gives the
  two level (0.98) at every gain in the sweep. That is the port's spatial footprint, not a
  calibration, and closing it would mean changing the multi-scale structure itself.
- SMB 1-1 overshoots its reference figures by about 3× in area and mean in both modes. V2-23
  established that the answer's own measurement of the same commit was 2.4× below this harness's
  figure for the same scene, so the two are not measuring the same frame.
